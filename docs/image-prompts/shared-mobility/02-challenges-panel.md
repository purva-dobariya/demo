# Key Challenges Sticky Panel

## Location on page
- Page: Shared Mobility (`Shared Mobility.html`)
- Section: "The Structural Reality" → "Key challenges" (`.challenges-wrap`)
- CSS target: `.challenges-img-panel img`
- Order: 2nd image placeholder on this page requiring a new photo

## Role in the page
This image sits in a sticky panel beside the vertical list of five challenge cards: high commission structures, fluctuating driver incomes, platform dependency, opaque pricing, and limited regional scale. As the reader scrolls down the challenge list, this single image stays pinned in view — it must function as a visual summary of "the structural burden of driving for a living today," reinforcing all five pain points at once (money leaking out, unpredictability, being boxed into one app) rather than illustrating just one.

## Display context
- Shape: Portrait, aspect-ratio 3:4
- Recommended output size: ~1500x2000px
- Presentation: Rendered at 85% opacity directly on a dark near-black card background (no gradient/caption overlay on this one — the whole frame stays visible), sitting sticky next to the scrolling challenge list. Because there's no caption to accommodate, compose for full-frame visual interest, but keep it moody/dark enough to sit comfortably beside the dark `.reality` section's other content.

## Subject & scene
A tight, driver's-eye-view shot looking across the dashboard and front seat of a car or auto-rickshaw during a break: two or three phones propped in different mounts/holders each showing a different blurred, generic ride-hailing map or fare screen (implying the driver toggles between several apps to piece together a living), a small handwritten notebook or ledger open on the passenger seat with scrawled tallies of trips and cash earned, a scatter of coins and small currency notes in the cup-holder or on the seat, and a worn fare-meter or phone holder bracket visible at the edge of frame. The arrangement should feel like an honest, cluttered "office" that exists only between rides — not a styled flat-lay.

## Composition & framing
A steep, over-the-shoulder or three-quarter high angle looking down and across the dashboard/seat area (not a perfectly flat overhead lay), filling the 3:4 frame edge-to-edge with detail so it reads richly even when small in a sticky sidebar. Slight desaturation and a lower-key exposure suit the dark section it sits in — this should look deliberately moodier and lower-lit than a bright daylight shot, since it has no gradient help from CSS.

## People
Optional: a single hand (the driver's) entering from the edge of frame, resting on the wheel or reaching to tap one of the mounted phones — hand only, no face needed, to keep focus on the cluttered dashboard "evidence" of the five challenges.

## Lighting & color
Directional, slightly moody lighting — window-filtered daylight from one side mixed with the cool glow of the phone screens, creating real shadow between the dashboard clutter rather than flat even light. Natural, true color temperature (worn vinyl greys and browns, faded rupee notes, warm phone-screen light) — do not add a blue color cast; keep it neutral/true-to-life so the section's existing dark-navy CSS background and radial glow provide the color mood.

## Mood
Overworked but methodical, financially stretched, real

## Must avoid
Styled, pristine "flat lay influencer" aesthetic; any real, recognizable ride-hailing app logos or brand marks on the phone screens; legible text on notebooks, ledgers, or fare meters; overly staged symmetry; HDR oversaturation; AI artifacts in hand anatomy or phone-screen distortion; non-Indian currency, script, or dashboard conventions.
