## Role in the page
This is a sticky portrait image running alongside the vertical list of five challenges: "Disconnected systems," "Inconsistent ticketing UX," "High integration overhead," "Limited discoverability," and "Dependence on physical ticketing." As the reader scrolls down the challenge list, this single image stays pinned beside it, so it needs to summarize the whole cluster of pain points in one frame — the tension between old, manual, physical-ticketing habits and the fragmented digital present, without illustrating any single bullet too literally.

- Recommended output size: ~1500x2000px
- Presentation: Rendered at 85% opacity, sticky-positioned (stays in view while the challenge list scrolls past it on desktop; stacks above the list on mobile). No caption or text overlay sits on this image, but it lives in a very dark section, so shadow detail should still read clearly at reduced opacity.

## Subject & scene
A bus-stand or bus-depot ticket window at a state road transport corporation depot: a conductor or ticket clerk behind a worn wooden/metal counter, handing a strip of paper tickets through a small window to a waiting commuter whose hand is reaching in with cash, while a small queue of two or three more commuters waits behind, one glancing impatiently at her phone. The scene should read as the literal bottleneck of physical-only ticketing and queues that the challenge list describes — worn paint, a hand-written fare chart taped to the window, a ceiling fan overhead, everyday depot clutter.

## Composition & framing
Portrait 3:4, camera positioned just behind and to the side of the waiting queue, looking toward the ticket window so the counter, the clerk's hands, and the first commuter's reaching hand are all legible in the middle of the frame; the queued commuters recede slightly out of focus behind. Keep the composition believable and unstaged — a slight downward tilt as if the photographer is standing in the queue themselves.

## People
Two to four people: a middle-aged male ticket clerk/conductor in a plain khaki or blue-grey transport-corporation-style uniform (no real corporation logo/name), handing over paper tickets; a woman commuter in everyday clothing (kurta or salwar-kameez) reaching to receive tickets and pay; one or two more commuters waiting behind, one checking a phone impatiently. All caught mid-action, not posed or smiling at the camera.

## Lighting & color
Practical daylight through an open depot bay or window, mixed with a bit of warm incandescent glow from an old bulb over the ticket counter — slightly dim, slightly dusty-feeling, true to a working depot's real lighting. Keep colors natural and a little muted (worn paint, faded signage colors) — no blue tint; the surrounding dark section applies its own overlay separately.

## Mood
Bureaucratic friction, patient waiting, everyday grind

## Must avoid
Any real transport-corporation name, logo, or route signage; overly clean/staged depot set; posed smiling clerk or commuters; legible fare amounts or ticket text; over-saturated HDR color; AI artifacts in hands exchanging tickets/cash; empty or sparse queue (must show at least a small huddle waiting).
