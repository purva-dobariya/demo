# Design System: ONDC Category Pages

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Dotted Canvas"**

Every ONDC sector — retail, food, logistics, transport, mobility, tourism, credit, investments, general trade, grassroots commerce — gets the same page, told in its own words. A dotted paper ground carries white panels; imagery moves in slow marquees rather than sitting in static grids; and a single blue line descends a vertical spine as the reader scrolls, colouring the mechanism into existence one word at a time.

The system is deliberately narrative. It refuses the marketing-page instinct to shout each feature at equal volume, and instead moves the reader through six fixed beats: the promise, the proof, the participants, the honest problem, the mechanism, and the invitation. Because the beats never change, a visitor who has read one sector page already knows how to read the next; because the copy, imagery and figures are entirely per-sector, no two pages feel templated.

This system is authoritative for the ten category pages under `pages/`. It governs the page body only: the shared site navbar, sector rail and footer keep the site's own Inter-on-navy chrome, and the boundary between the two is intentional and visible.

**Key characteristics:**

- A dotted off-white canvas grounds floating white panels with generous radius.
- Type is one family at one voice — Space Grotesk, tight tracking, heavy display weights against plain body copy.
- Emphasis is carried by *weight reversal*: the emphasised phrase in a headline is lighter, not heavier.
- Motion is continuous and ambient — marquees loop, a spine fills, words take colour — never a bounce or a pop.
- Figures are large, unadorned and animate once; no icons anywhere.
- Imagery is always in motion or behind a veil; it never sits still in a rectangle.

## Colors

A near-monochrome ground so a single blue can carry all connection, progress and emphasis.

### Primary

- **Network Blue (`#2151F5`):** The spine, the active node, links, emphasis inside body copy, and the primary hover state. It is the only saturated colour on the page.
- **Figure Blue (`#1877F2`):** The unit or qualifier beside a statistic ("+ sellers", "lower"). Slightly softer than network-blue so the numeral stays dominant.

### Neutral

- **Canvas (`#f7f8f8`):** The page ground, always carrying the 32px dot grid.
- **Surface (`#ffffff`):** Panels and cards that float above the canvas.
- **Ink (`#1b1b1b`) / Ink Deep (`#191919`):** Body text and headlines respectively.
- **Ink Figure (`#0d1b2e`):** Statistics only — a cooler, denser black that holds up at display size.
- **Muted (`#55595e`) / Quiet (`#8a8f95`):** Supporting copy and card metadata.
- **Line (`#e6e7e9`) / Line Soft (`#ececec`):** Grid rules and list separators.
- **Ghost (`#eef0f3`):** The oversized step numerals behind the mechanism spine.

### Named Rules

**The Single Blue Rule.** One blue explains connection, progress and emphasis. A page that needs a second accent colour is a page whose hierarchy has failed.

**The Canvas Is Never Flat Rule.** The dot grid is not decoration — it is what makes the white panels read as floating. Never ship the canvas colour without it.

**The Figures Own Weight Rule.** Statistics are the only place display weight appears outside a headline. Nothing else competes at that size.

## Typography

**One family: Space Grotesk** (400 / 600 / 700, self-hosted in `fx.css`, with a metric-matched fallback). Weight 500 resolves to 400 by design rather than pulling a fourth face over the wire.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** — the hero headline, once per page.
- **Beat** — the three-line mechanism headline, staggered down and across.
- **Headline** — section headings.
- **Title** — mechanism step titles and role panel headings.
- **Figure** — statistics. Word-statistics ("Shared", "Unsecured") drop one step so they sit level with numerals.
- **Body** — every paragraph, at a generous 1.7 line height.
- **Label** — eyebrows and card kinds: 13px, 600, wide tracking, uppercase.

### Named Rules

**The Weight Reversal Rule.** Emphasis inside a headline is expressed by dropping to weight 400 (`<em>`), not by raising to bold. The eye lands on the lighter phrase because everything around it is heavy — the opposite of the convention, and the reason the headlines read as editorial rather than promotional.

**The Tight Tracking Rule.** Display sizes carry roughly `-.035em`. Space Grotesk sets loose at scale; without the negative tracking the headlines read as a slide deck.

**The No Icon Rule.** There are no icons in this system. A concept that needs an icon to be understood needs better words.

## Layout

- Panels are inset from the canvas by 32px and sit on a 28px radius.
- The content column caps at 1440px; the mechanism section caps tighter at 1200px.
- Section padding runs `clamp(24px, 5vw, 84px)` horizontally — the canvas breathes more than the panels do.
- Asymmetry is structural: the participant rail is `1fr / 1.25fr`, reality is `1.1fr / 1fr`, and mechanism steps alternate a 14% indent. Nothing is centred except the hero and the closing invitation.
- Breakpoints: 1000px collapses every two-column grid to one and hides the floating orbs; 700px drops the statistics to a single column and one marquee track.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes from stacking and blur, never from heavy shadows.

- **Cards:** `0 4px 16px rgba(0,0,0,.05)`, lifting to `0 16px 38px rgba(0,0,0,.10)` on hover with a 3px rise.
- **Marquee media:** `0 18px 44px rgba(0,0,0,.13)` — the only genuinely deep shadow, because those cards are meant to feel like they are passing behind the page.
- **The hero veil:** a radial gradient of the canvas colour that fades the moving imagery out behind the headline. This is the system's signature move — the images are never dimmed with black, they are *dissolved into the paper*.

## Shapes

- 16px on participant cards, 22px on marquee media, 28px on panels, fully round on every control.
- The step nodes and floating orbs are the only circles.
- Orbs carry a dotted 1.2px outline at 12px offset — the same dot language as the canvas, at a different scale.

## Motion

Motion is ambient and continuous rather than triggered and showy.

- **Marquees** run 42–48s linear and infinite, pausing on hover. They exist to make the page feel alive at rest.
- **The living network** (About page only) — a Canvas-2D map of India that grows node-by-node in proportion to scroll, in the single blue on the light canvas. Under reduced motion it renders once, fully formed, with no animation loop.
- **The homepage hero** — the one place in the system that is dark, and it stays dark end to end. The ground is `hero-void`, a deep navy rather than a black, so it belongs to the blue the network is drawn in; a very low radial lift (`hero-lift-core`, `hero-lift-edge`) keeps it from reading as a dead rectangle, which is the dark-surface equivalent of the canvas dot grid. The page therefore has exactly one hard cut — dark hero, then the light canvas for everything below it. A light-developing variant was built and rejected in review: the cut reads stronger than the crossfade.
  Within the hero the network is one blue family: edges take `network-blue` itself, particles a lit tint (`hero-particle`) so a single dot still registers against the navy. Blending is **additive** — correct on a permanently dark ground, where dense parts of the map are meant to bloom — and the particle halo stays for the same reason. `--hero-spine` (0→1 across the act) drives the story's spine; landmark washes on the metric tiles and the swish across the lockup use blue values read for a dark ground rather than the paper tint the light sections use. Under reduced motion everything renders finished: map formed, spine drawn, landmarks landed, nothing moving.
  The map never leaves the blue screen: `.ondc-hero` is `overflow: clip` (clip, never hidden — hidden would make it a scroll container and unstick the stage), and `--hero-exit`, written by the impact choreography over the act's last half-viewport, dissolves the canvas before the clip edge could cut it. The strip that follows the hero always paints opaque paper — it is the cover the layout depends on, never transparent.
- **The homepage's light half** carries its own quiet motion system, all under `body.home`:
  - `.hv-rv` reveals — sections observe themselves once and rise their children with inline stagger delays; after the entrance a `.hv-done` class retires the delays so they can never postpone hover transitions through a React re-render. Final state under reduced motion.
  - **Metric cells** take the stat-cell wash (`blue-wash`, scaleY from the bottom on hover) and count up once on first reveal (1200ms cubic-out).
  - **The building-blocks deck** — the section's memorable move: cards in a native horizontal scroll rail are `position: sticky` on the inline axis, so passed cards pin at the track's left edge and each next card slides over them; the card taking the front gets a one-shot blue sheen (`homeCardSwish`) and passed cards scale to .955. Ghost step numerals (`numeral-ghost` step, `ghost` colour) sit behind each card's copy; even cards flip their media side; the two imageless blocks sit on a blue-dotted ghost surface. No scroll-jacking — arrows, dots and snap all survive.
  - **The chapter radial** — "The Network in Action" opens under a soft blue radial (`rgba(33,81,245,.08)` → `blue-wash` → transparent) so scrolling into the stories reads as arriving somewhere new; the story cards hang from blue nodes on hairline drops beneath it.
  - **The ambient network** — a second, small mesh-engine instance (the engine's own `voices-ambient` drift state) on a fixed canvas behind the light sections at 38% opacity, recoloured to `network-blue` on paper. It runs only while the light half is on screen, blanks its bitmap when paused (a lingering frame would paint over the hero), bursts its pulse rate as each section header enters — the background answers the scroll — and sits out reduced motion entirely. It never touches the hero's meshBus.
- **The spine** fills in proportion to scroll position; nodes take colour as it passes them.
- **Word scrub** colours the mechanism copy word by word as the block crosses the viewport — reading is the animation.
- **Entrances** rise 26px and fade over 0.7s.

### Named Rules

**The No Overshoot Rule.** Every entrance uses `cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)` — fast attack, long settle, no bounce past the resting position. Spring and elastic curves are not part of this system.

**The Reduced Motion Rule.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, every marquee, float and scrub stops and all content renders in its final state. The page loses its ambience, never its meaning.

## Components

### Buttons

Pills only. Solid ink for the primary action, inverting to network blue on hover; bordered ghost on translucent white for the secondary. Both at `16px 34px`.

### Panels

White, 28px radius, inset 32px from the canvas. The numbers, reality and join sections are panels; the hero, participant rail and mechanism sit directly on the canvas. This alternation is what gives the page its rhythm.

### Statistics grid

Three columns, hairline ruled, with a blue wash that scales up from the bottom on hover. Cell one — and only cell one — carries a masked dot texture. Six figures read cleanest; nine work without modification.

### Participant cards

Kind label, centred logo, then name and one line above a hairline. Heights vary between 285 and 310px so the two marquee columns never fall into step.

### Participant directory

Buyer and seller role pages use a directory rather than the category narrative. A compact role statement and live count hand directly into a white ledger on the dotted canvas. The ledger aggregates repeated registry rows into one organisation card, exposes real category, channel and node metadata, and paginates at 24 entities so the interface remains responsive at network scale.

Directory cards are logo-led, non-clickable records: uppercase category, a fixed logo field with a typographic fallback, participant name and one quiet metadata line above a hairline. The grid runs four, three, two and one columns as space narrows. Search and select controls use 12px corners rather than pills; pagination and actions remain fully round. Directory motion is limited to the live-count entrance and ordinary reveals so filtering never competes with ambient movement.

### Mechanism spine

A hairline track with a blue fill, numbered nodes, oversized ghost numerals, and steps that alternate indentation. Four to six steps.

### Role tabs

A pill group on a grey field; the active tab inverts to ink. All panels ship in the markup so the section degrades to a plain stack without JavaScript.

### About page components

The About page (`pages/about-us.html`) is an institutional narrative, not a category argument. It speaks the same language with its own shapes, all defined in the "About page" section of `beckn.css`:

- **Hero photo (`.bk-hero-photo`)** — hero variant 3: a single still photograph behind the radial veil, dissolved into the paper. No marquee, no dark gradient.
- **Journey timeline (`.bk-tl` family)** — the page's signature move. One vertical hairline (`.bk-tl-years::before`) the years hang off; year numerals reuse the figure step in ink-figure; entries and milestone beats are hairline-ruled rows carrying a whisper of the canvas colour (`rgba(247,248,248,.8)`) so copy stays readable over the map. Behind it, a sticky Canvas-2D "living network" map of India grows node-by-node with scroll — single blue nodes and edges, resting-grey tints (`node-rest`, `quiet`), and a neutral ink coastline at `rgba(27,27,27,.12–.18)`. The 500M finale reuses the numeral-ghost size step at weight 700 in ink-figure, counted up via `data-bk-count`.
- **Spine fill and landmarks (`.bk-tl-years::after`, `.bk-tl-wash`, `.bk-tl-beat::before`)** — the hairline is redrawn in blue as the reader descends, scaled via a `--tl-fill` custom property written per frame by the page's scroll loop (the line never changes size). Each transaction milestone announces itself twice: a node on the spine takes the blue and pops (the `.bk-step-node` mechanism, square instead of round), and a `#e4eefc` wash sweeps the row from the left on `scaleX` — the `.bk-stat-fill` mechanism turned on its side. The 500M finale takes the same wash at block scale on a 28px radius. All of it renders finished under reduced motion.
- **Board cards (`.bk-people` / `.bk-person`)** — 22px-radius portrait cards, four to a row, capped at 260px because the source photographs are ~200px wide. The caption sits *off* the portrait: the org eyebrow rides above it on white, and name and role sit below it over a hairline. The portrait itself carries no overlay at all — nothing is laid over a face, so the picture stays whole and the type stays readable on plain paper. The two solo directors centre on their own rows, opening and closing the board.
- **Founder rows (`.bk-inst-row`)** — institutions as hairline-ruled rows, eyebrow + name + line.
- **Wall of institutions (`.bk-inst-logos`)** — the one supplied logo-grid artwork composed rather than dropped in: a white panel floating on the dotted canvas, carrying its own header row (eyebrow left, counted-up figure right) above a hairline, with the artwork's side margins feathered into the panel so no raw rectangle shows.
- **Chronology (`.bk-chrono`, `.bk-award`)** — the recognitions in the order they arrived. Each year is a group: its numeral holds in `node-rest` beside the rows while they scroll past, and each group steps a further 4% in, so the stack reads as a descent through time rather than a wall of equal cards. Rows are hairline-ruled with a square 16px-radius thumbnail, and hover tints to canvas and translates the children 12px — the `.bk-barrier` mechanism. The featured recognition above keeps full size and opens the same lightbox.
- **Award lightbox (`.bk-lightbox`)** — the clicked card FLIP-expands to a centred 28px-radius white panel with `--bk-ease-out`; the backdrop fades the page to the canvas colour (`rgba(247,248,248,.85)` + blur) — to paper, never to black. Close is a ghost-pill text button.
- **Newsletter rows (`.bk-nl-row`)** — the library shape as ruled link-rows: kind eyebrow, title, one line, and a `.bk-ul` download link. The `·` separator is the only mark.
- **Mask utility (`.bk-fade-b`)** — bottom-dissolve `mask-image` for imagery that must melt into its surface.
- **Role procedures (`.bk-relay`, `.bk-service-plane`)** — role pages use the shared dotted canvas but take their structure from the role: Gateway becomes a three-step relay ledger, while Technology Service Provider becomes a service continuum. Both use one blue hairline system, oversized light numerals, and no iconography.
- **Resource library (`.bk-library`)** — the Resources hub alone turns search into the primary action, followed by a dynamically rendered ruled collection index. Resource-detail pages retain their existing knowledge-base interface.
- **Correspondence form (`.bk-contact`)** — Contact treats fields as ruled correspondence rows, with text-led office information and the existing mail handoff; inputs, errors, and focus use the single blue rule.

### Careers page components

The Careers page (`pages/careers.html`) is the invitation shape — culture, then a list of openings — and the liveliest page in the system while staying inside every rule. Its components live in the "Careers page" section of `beckn.css`:

- **Open-seats ledger (`.bk-seats`, `.bk-seat`, `.bk-seat-row`, `.bk-seat-jd`)** — the signature move. Each open role is a native `<details>` hairline row (`name="bk-seat"` gives one-open-at-a-time for free). Open/close is an instant layout change — the same answer the role tabs give, because nothing in this system animates height — and the revealed JD plays a transform-and-opacity entrance (`bkSeatIn`) instead. Row hover reuses the barrier mechanism: canvas tint, children translate 12px. Without JavaScript every row stays visible and openable.
- **Seat node (`.bk-seat-node`)** — the "empty seat in the network" motif: a hollow network-blue circle beside each role that fills solid on hover and on `[open]`. Background and border only — paint transitions, never size.
- **Constellation (`.bk-const`)** — decorative inline SVG beside the section heading, `aria-hidden`: hairline edges in the line colour, filled "taken" seats in `node-rest`, and one hollow network-blue circle per open role breathing on an opacity-only pulse (~3.2s, staggered). The hollow-node count must match the ledger. Renders finished under reduced motion; hidden at 700px.
- **Track filter (`.bk-seat-filter`)** — the role-tab pill restated for one-to-many filtering, active on `aria-pressed` so `beckn.js`'s tab handler leaves it alone. Filtering toggles `hidden` on rows and dims non-matching constellation nodes (`.bk-dim`) — it never animates layout.
- **Hero tally (`.bk-careers-tally`)** — one quiet line under the hero actions carrying the page's single count-up (`data-bk-count`) in the blue.
- **Culture grid (`.bk-culture`)** and **life grid (`.bk-life`)** — asymmetric two-column grids (`.9fr/1.1fr` and `1.15fr/1fr`). The culture section borrows `id="bk-mech"` so the shared script initialises its word scrub; there is no spine and the spine code bails safely. Life blurbs are hairline-ruled blocks beside photographs dissolved into the panel with `.bk-fade-b` — no marquee, since fewer than five images exist and the loop seam rule forbids short marquees.
- **RFP ledger (`.bk-rfp-row`)** — the tender list as a record, not a filtered search: the `.bk-barrier` hover mechanism restated with the due date carrying the single blue, and closed tenders staying visible (just quieter) inside their own tab rather than disappearing. `.bk-tabs`/`.bk-tab` switch Open/Closed/Empanelled; the hero tally is a manual count-up (the total is data-driven, so it can't use `data-bk-count`'s static target) built from the same cubic ease-out.
- **Holding node (`.bk-hold-status`, `.bk-hold-node`)** — the quietest page on the site: one hollow blue circle on a hairline, reusing the constellation's `bkSeatPulse` breathing rather than a bespoke canvas rig. No marquee, no spine, no count-up — a single ambient signal that the capability is connected but not switched on.
- **Legal document (`.bk-legal-head`, `.bk-doc-index`, `.bk-clause`, `numeral-ghost-sm`)** — a reading instrument, not a landing page: no hero image, no invented figures. `.bk-clause` blocks carry a faint gutter numeral (the `numeral-ghost-sm` step, `clamp(72px, 9vw, 132px)` — a restrained sibling of `numeral-ghost` sized for clause-dense body copy rather than a mechanism spine). Longer documents get a sticky `.bk-doc-index` whose active link tracks scroll position in blue; terms-and-conditions instead uses `.bk-clause-toc`, a numbered ledger, since its clauses are already numbered in the source.
- **Participant ledger (`.bk-np-page`, `.bk-np-table`)** — network-participants and ecosystem-participants publish the same registry and are joined by one tab pill, so they are one screen in two files: the same page frame (`.bk-role-page` + `.bk-np-page`), the same hero, the same ledger, the same controls. The data is genuinely tabular, so on these two pages the shape is a ruled table rather than a logo-card grid: hairline row dividers, no cell borders, canvas tint on hover, blue only where it earns it (links, the count). A plate-grid candidate shipped beside them for a time and has been withdrawn: the question it was asked to settle — whether the registry is read by scanning rows against each other down a column, or by finding one organisation and reaching it — was answered for the column, which is what a table is for.

  The sheet's seven columns were not the ledger's. They gave the logo, a raw URL and a column empty in 123 of 150 rows the same standing as the participant's name, and wrapped "Technology Service Provider (TSP)-Seller" — three times over in some rows — into a column barely wider than the phrase, which ran rows from 85px to 271px and pushed the table wider than the page. Four rules fix that, and they are the ledger's design:

  - **Four columns, fixed shares.** Participant / Role / Categories / Contact, at 29 / 12 / 31 / 28 written into a `<colgroup>` by the page under `table-layout: fixed`, so one participant with seven categories cannot take the width away from every other row. Logo and name are one cell (`.bk-np-who` / `.bk-np-whoin`); contact and website are one cell of actions (`Name · Email · Call · Website`), not three labelled lines and a URL.
  - **Role is a key, never a phrase.** `.bk-np-key` prints BNP, SNP, TSP-B, TSP-S, TSP-RSF, TSP-IGM as an `<abbr>` — typography, not a badge: no fill, no border, a dotted underline only on hover. The map lives in `npCsv.ROLE_KEYS`; an unmapped role falls through as its own text, so next month's CSV cannot lose a row.
  - **The filter is the legend.** Role left the sidebar for a row of `.bk-np-role` toggles above the results, each carrying the key and the words together (`BNP  Buyer NP  130`). That is what earns the table the right to print BNP alone: the expansion is always already on screen, which also covers touch, where an `abbr` title never opens.
  - **A column of dashes is never a column.** A `VIEW` entry with a `field` is drawn only when some row fills it, and the two sparse fields — Technology Service Provider, Live NP supported — are printed under the participant's name as `.bk-np-enabled` in the rows that carry them instead. The CSV export is unaffected: `COLUMNS` stays the sheet verbatim, in sheet order, and is also the search index, so a full role phrase or a raw URL is still typeable.

  Categories keep their real names — they are the content, and abbreviating them would cost more than it saves. Three show, the rest sit behind a `.bk-np-more` `+N` inside the row; separators are `::after` on the chip so a wrapped line never opens with a stranded `·`.

  The sidebar is the taxonomy alone: the official tree from `filters-ondc-participants-sheet.csv`, narrowed to what the active list actually holds, every branch expanding in place. A category or sub-category with nothing behind it is left out, not listed and greyed: greyed out asserted "no participants", when the truth was "none in this list" — most of what read as empty on Ecosystem Participants is well populated on Network Participants, and the two Network tabs carry different sets again. A category the list has no sub-categories for keeps its checkbox and loses its chevron, since an expander onto an empty box is a promise the branch cannot keep.

  The hero is compact by rule, and it carries no live count. The count went from a ruled panel of its own, to a sentence at the `figure` step beside the actions, to nothing: the result count above the results (`role="status"`) already states the total, and a hero figure was a second copy of it in the most expensive space on the page. The second headline the directory used to open with is gone for the same reason. Search and the Key/Integrated switch share one row.

  **The Count Is Stated Once Rule.** A directory's total belongs where a reader is already looking — on the results, beside the range and the filters that change it. A hero figure cannot move when a filter narrows the list, so it is either redundant or wrong. The result count is the only place the number is stated; pagination carries the control, never a second copy of it.

  On a phone the row becomes a record: `thead` goes screen-reader-only, each `td` is a block with its column name from `data-label` in the eyebrow style, and the `+N` retires because a one-column record has room for the whole list. Nothing scrolls sideways. `payment-assurance-program-approved.html` still borrows the bare `.bk-np-table`, so every rule above is scoped to `.bk-np-page` and the registers keep their pinned-column scroller.
- **Buyer-app grid (`.bk-shop-step`, `.bk-app-grid`)** — how-to-shop's memorable move: the step number fills the entire left column at the `numeral-ghost` step, the instruction sitting in the gutter beside it; no nodes, no spine (complaint owns that vocabulary). The hero marquee is earned with real buyer-app logos (`.bk-rail-cols`/`.bk-card`, the participant-rail kit) rather than invented stock photography.

### Payment Assurance Program components

The Payment Assurance Program page (`pages/payment-assurance-program.html`) is a reading instrument that ends in a register: a voluntary deposit scheme whose binding document is a Notification, and whose operative artifact is two published lists. It borrows the legal-document frame and fills the right-hand column with instruments rather than prose. Its components live in the "Payment Assurance Program" section of `beckn.css`:

- **Wide document body (`.bk-pap-body`)** — the `.bk-legal-body` grid retuned for a column that carries two registers, two step ledgers and four paired lists: `minmax(196px, .22fr) / minmax(0, .78fr)` at a 1320px cap, against the legal pages' `.28fr / .72fr` at 1160px. Prose inside still caps at `.bk-clause`'s own 640/680px measure, so only the instruments spend the extra width. It collapses to one column at 900px in its own media block — the shared collapse rule is declared earlier in the file and would otherwise lose.
- **The index as a gauge (`.bk-pap-index`, `.bk-pap-gauge`)** — the page's signature move and the reason it is not privacy-policy with different words. The sticky index draws its own hairline rail, and a blue fill descends it in proportion to reading progress, so the document's navigation reads as a *level* — the deposit the page is about. One hairline, `scaleY` off `--bk-pap-read`, never a height animation; the same grammar as `.bk-relay-steps::after` and `.bk-tl-years::after`. The element takes no `position` of its own at desktop: `.bk-doc-index` is already sticky, and sticky is positioned, so the rail and the gauge anchor to it — forcing `relative` would turn its inherited `top: 120px` into a paint offset and drop the index onto the clauses. Below 900px, where the shared index goes static, it takes `relative` with `top: 0` so the rail keeps an anchor; there the index becomes a chip row and the gauge turns on its side rather than switching off: the same `--bk-pap-read` scales a hairline along the bottom of the chip row on `scaleX`. The page's one remembered move is not a desktop feature.

The enrolment sequence in the same page deliberately stays static `.bk-shop-step` ghost numerals, so the two step ledgers never rhyme.
- **Paired ruled lists (`.bk-pap-cols`)** — what you get against what it asks; what the Badge tells you against what it does not. Asymmetric at `1fr / 1.18fr`, a ratio the kit does not already spend. Ruled groups on this page (the glance ledger, the reason rows) carry `.bk-pap-cascade`, which gives each `.bk-rv` child a stepped transition-delay so a group arrives as a run rather than a block, and retires the delay on `.bk-in` so it can never postpone a hover. - **Registers (`.bk-pap-register`, `.bk-pap-tally`)** — two `.bk-np-table` ledgers in one clause, each with its own tally at the `figure` step, its status line, and `.bk-directory-input` / `.bk-directory-select` controls. Filtering rewrites the rows and the empty state is a row rather than a vanished table, so the header never hangs over nothing. The pulse is reserved for that standalone callout: in a six-row register two rows blinking indefinitely is a distraction the state does not need, so the hollow ring alone carries On Hold there.
- **Ruled link rows (`.bk-pap-rows`, `.bk-pap-pending`)** — documents and support as `.bk-barrier`-family rows with the hover translate. A destination that does not exist yet reads "— TBC" in muted text rather than shipping a dead anchor or a button that does nothing.

Contrast is scoped rather than raised site-wide: this page's eyebrows, paired-list column heads and tally labels come off the kit's quiet grey to muted (6.63:1 on the canvas), scoped to `.bk-pap-body`, its hero and its numbers head, so no other page's eyebrows move.

Its `.bk-requirement` rows drop the numeral track inside a clause (`minmax(0,.62fr) / minmax(0,1fr)`): three reasons are not three steps, and nothing there is ordered.

### Payment Assurance holding page

While the Programme waits to be published, `pages/payment-assurance-program.html` holds its URL — the link sits in 45 page footers and the React homepage footer, so it has to land somewhere real — and the finished page waits beside it as `-approved.html`, unchanged and still under test. `scripts/payment-assurance-golive.js` swaps them.

- **The words take the blue (`.bk-soon-word`, `bkSoonSweep`)** — the holding page's one move. "Coming soon" at the `role-display` step, each letterform in its own span, with a blue wave that travels across the word and releases on a 3.2s loop, staggered 0.1s per letter. It is the word scrub (`beckn.js` colours a block's words one at a time as it crosses the viewport) turned from a scroll-driven effect into an idle one: the same idea, no longer waiting on a reader. Colour only — no gradient text, no `background-clip`, nothing that moves or resizes. The rest between passes is deliberate: the sweep should arrive, not shimmer. Under reduced motion the animation is `none` and the headline renders finished in ink, never stranded mid-wave. The `<h1>` carries `aria-label`, the spans are `aria-hidden`, and a real space between the two word groups keeps the text content correct if the label is ever dropped.
- The page is one screen and nothing more: eyebrow, headline, status line, one paragraph. No actions, no closing row, no coda — a holding page has nothing to route a visitor to that the navbar and footer do not already carry, and the sweep reads louder with nothing competing beneath it. The only borrowed part is the `.bk-hold-status` / `.bk-hold-node` "not live" signal, reused verbatim from `pages/coming-soon.html`: a hairline with a hollow node breathing on `bkSeatPulse`. `pages/coming-soon.html` itself is untouched — the shared holding page stays deliberately quiet for the nine capabilities that route through it.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- Keep the six beats in order — promise, proof, participants, reality, mechanism, invitation.
- Let the canvas dots show; they are the ground the whole system stands on.
- Use `<em>` for headline emphasis and let the weight drop.
- Vary marquee card heights so the columns feel hand-placed.
- Name the honest problem in the reality section. The mechanism only lands because the barriers were stated plainly first.
- Give each sector its own imagery, figures and words. The structure repeats; the content never should.

### Don't:

- Don't add a second accent colour, or spend blue on decoration.
- Don't bold an emphasised phrase — that inverts the system's core typographic move.
- Don't introduce icons, illustrations or badges.
- Don't use bounce, spring or elastic easing anywhere.
- Don't let a marquee column hold fewer than five cards; the loop seam becomes visible.
- Don't animate width, height, padding or margin — scale and translate instead.
- Don't let this system leak into the shared navbar, sector rail or footer.
